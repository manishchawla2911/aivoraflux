In short RPA repeats fixed steps on screens and structured data, so it suits stable, rule-only tasks. AI can read unstructured documents such as PDFs, scans and emails, so it suits vendor onboarding, where every supplier sends documents in a different format. Many teams combine them: AI reads and checks, RPA or APIs enter the clean data.

What each one does

RPA (robotic process automation) is software that mimics a person clicking through applications: it copies values from one screen or file and enters them into another, following a script. It is reliable when the inputs and screens stay the same.

AI-based automation uses document AI and language models to understand content: it can recognise a trade licence in a scanned PDF, find the expiry date wherever it appears and decide whether it passes your rules. It can also say how confident it is.

When RPA is the right choice

  • The data is already structured, such as a web form or a CSV export.
  • The target system has no API and needs entries typed into screens.
  • The steps rarely change.

When AI is the right choice

  • Documents come from many suppliers in many formats.
  • The task involves reading and judging content, such as validity, expiry or name matching.
  • You need to know when the system is unsure, so a person can step in.

The combined approach

For vendor onboarding, a practical design is: AI reads each document, extracts and checks the fields, and routes exceptions to a reviewer; once approved, the clean data is written into the ERP through its API, or by RPA where no API exists. Each tool does the part it is good at.

Decision checklist

  1. Are your inputs mostly structured data? If yes, RPA may be enough.
  2. Do documents arrive as PDFs, scans or emails in varying layouts? Then you need AI for the reading.
  3. Does your target system have an API? If yes, use it; use RPA only where it doesn't.
  4. Do you need an audit trail of why each item was approved or flagged? Choose the design that records it.
